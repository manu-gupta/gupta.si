The AI story is also an infrastructure story. Power, networks, land and capital will shape how quickly intelligence can scale.

AI is physical

AI is often discussed as if it lives entirely inside software. In reality, every large AI system sits on physical infrastructure: chips, servers, data centres, cooling systems, fibre networks, electricity connections, land and capital.

The International Energy Agency estimates that data centres used about 415 TWh of electricity in 2024 and expects global data-centre electricity use to roughly double by 2030 in its base case. The exact number will change. The strategic point will not: software demand can grow faster than physical infrastructure can be built.

When compute becomes available, the bottleneck often moves somewhere else: power, cooling, networks, land or finance.

The bottleneck moves

When compute becomes more available, another constraint can appear. It may be grid capacity, transformers, cooling equipment, land, network connectivity or financing. AI infrastructure should therefore be studied as a chain rather than as a single GPU number.

What this means for capital

The durable opportunities may sit in supporting layers: power, data-centre construction, cooling, networking, security and specialised infrastructure. The AI story is therefore also an industrial and infrastructure story.
